Values to Behaviours Kit

Three tools to turn each value into three things people can see. Staff can act on them. Managers can judge them.

01

At a glance

What it is

Values you can see

Most firms have values on the wall. Few staff can say what they mean on a busy day.

A value like "Customer first" is too big to act on. People need to know what to do at the counter, on the call, or in the meeting.

This kit turns each value into three clear actions and two things to avoid. Each one comes from a real story in your firm.

Who it is for

Who it is for

HR heads, HRBPs, culture and L&D teams, and the managers who live the values each day.

When to use it

Three moments

  • You have new values and need them to stick.
  • Staff roll their eyes when values come up.
  • Reviews ask about values, but no one knows how to rate them.

1
Step 1 of 5

Collect real stories

Week 1

The HRBP leads this, with two or three managers.

  1. Ask staff for a time someone lived the value.
  2. Write down what the person did, step by step.
  3. Get at least five stories for each value.

Step 2 of 5

Write the do and don't

Week 2

The HRBP writes it. The value owner checks it.

  1. Find what the people in the stories did again and again.
  2. Write three do lines and two don't lines.
  3. Use the words staff use at work.

Step 3 of 5

Ask what it means for managers

Week 3

Each manager fills this in. The HRBP runs a short session.

  1. Pick two or three things the manager will do each week.
  2. Say how the team will notice them.
  3. Write what the manager will do when they fall short.

Step 4 of 5

Test with 5 staff

Week 4

The HRBP does this, with five staff from different teams.

  1. Read each line to five staff.
  2. Ask: could you do this tomorrow? Could your manager see it?
  3. Fix or drop any line that two people find unclear.

03

Example and how-to

Filled-in example · Behaviour Canvas

Customer first

A bike service centre chain with 14 outlets in Mumbai. This is an example. Copy the shape, not the words.

Value
Customer first
Do 1
Give an accurate estimate before we start.
Do 2
Call the customer before any extra repair.
Do 3
Explain a mistake honestly, and say how we will fix it.
Don't 1
Add a part or a repair without asking.
Don't 2
Blame the rider for a fault we caused.
Real story
At the Andheri outlet, Ravi found a worn chain. He called the owner before he fixed it. She said yes. Next month she brought her brother's bike too.
Small touch
Offer tea while the customer waits.
How-to guide · 3 minutes

How to turn a value into 3 behaviours

  1. Pick one value.
  2. Ask staff for a time someone lived it.
  3. Find what that person did. Write it as an action.
  4. Write three do lines and two don't lines.
  5. Check that a manager could see each one.
  6. Test the lines with five staff, then fix them.
