The Chanakya problem

Chanakya stepped aside. Given the chance, would your CHRO step forward?

चाणक्यThe parallel · Chanakya and Chandragupta

More than 2,000 years ago, Chanakya understood ambition, loyalty and power better than anyone in the Mauryan court. He found Chandragupta, trained him, and made him king. Then he stepped aside. We remember Chanakya as a brilliant strategist. We don't remember him as a king.

Did he choose that? Or was the throne never really his to take?

Today's CHRO is in the same spot. They understand power better than most people in the building. They just rarely get to hold it.

The pattern we do not talk about

A woman heads HR in 1 in 3 large Indian companies. Count how many run the business.

About 1 in 3 large Indian companies has a woman heading HR. Only around 5% of listed companies have a woman CEO.

~1 in 3large Indian companies with a woman heading HR
~5%of listed companies with a woman CEO

On its own, the first number looks like progress. Put the 2 side by side and it tells a different story.

Women are being welcomed into the 1 C-suite role that rarely leads to the top. The roles that do lead there, like P&L, operations and finance, are still mostly held by men. So the CHRO-to-CEO gap isn't just a business problem. It's a gender problem too, hidden inside a number we like to celebrate.

The bias sticks

This bias didn't come from nowhere. That's why it's so hard to shake off.

"Personnel" departments were set up to run payroll, keep the company legal, and handle hiring and firing paperwork. In India, that sometimes even meant organising the Diwali rangoli. In the 1990s, Dave Ulrich pushed hard for HR to be seen as a business partner and a change agent. Decades later, the old image is still around.

The ladder HR builds for everyone else

If this were only about skills, the fix would be easy: send CHROs to business school and wait. It isn't that simple.

Most CHROs don't own a P&L, and that's by design. HR exists to support the parts of the business that make money. That keeps HR efficient. But it also means the CHRO never gets to run a business of their own, and that's the first thing a board looks for in a CEO.

ToolHR P&L WorkbookBring your own numbers when the room talks money.

It's the same in the boardroom. The CFO is at every board meeting and every investor call, and learns over the years how to handle a board. The CHRO is called in when there's something on the agenda for them, like pay or succession, and leaves when it's done.

From practiceWho is in the boardroom, and how often

And this is the irony. The CHRO usually builds the succession plan, including the one for the next CEO. They spend years working out what makes a good leader and measuring everyone else against it. Hardly anyone measures them against it.

They make the kings, but they're never considered for the throne.

It isn't ability

I've seen HR leaders run businesses well. At EY's People Advisory Services, leading a service line means owning the numbers, and many of those leaders came from HR. I've seen the same at Darwinbox.

From practiceEY People Advisory Services, Darwinbox

But I've also sat in board meetings where the HR head went silent once the conversation turned to numbers. And several CEOs have told me, one on one, "I wish my HR team understood the business better."

Both are true, and that's the hard part.

The curse can be broken: a view from my own campus

When did your HR head last speak first on a number in the boardroom?

I don't need to look far for proof. 5 people who broke the curse are XLRI alumni, like me.

  • Leena Nair went from Unilever's global CHRO to Global CEO of Chanel in 2022.
  • Pushp Kumar Joshi went from Director (HR) at HPCL to Chairman and Managing Director of HPCL in 2022.
  • George Verghese went from heading HR at the Kirloskar Group to Managing Director of Kirloskar Industries in 2025.
  • Deepak Kumar Hota spent 30 years at HPCL, including heading its natural gas business, then joined BEML as Director (HR) and became its CMD 3 years later.
  • Tanuj Kapilashrami went from CHRO of Standard Chartered to Chief Strategy and Talent Officer, and then Group Chief Operating Officer.
ExamplesXLRI alumni who moved from HR to the top job

They got there in 2 ways. Deepak and Tanuj added business roles to their HR experience. Leena, Pushp and George went straight from the CHRO's chair to the top job. Either way, their boards saw them as business leaders, not just HR leaders.

So the top job isn't out of reach for HR. It's just rarely offered, and even more rarely asked for.

So here's the question

Boards listen in value, cost and profit. What did your last people problem cost the business?
